# Evaluation of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in Mitigating Large Language Model Hallucinations

## Executive Summary

This report synthesizes available research evidence regarding the effectiveness of Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in reducing hallucinations in Large Language Models (LLMs). The findings present a clear dichotomy: empirically, RAG acts as a significant operational mitigation tool, notably reducing hallucination rates in specific tasks such as structured workflow and table generation (e.g., from 21% down to less than 7.5%). Theoretically and structurally, however, RAG is not a complete cure. Because RAG only improves semantic relevance, models can still misinterpret context or generate inaccuracies around the retrieved source material. RAG is thus best understood as an effective mitigation strategy rather than a mathematical or structural solution to the underlying reasoning limitations of LLMs.

## Research Question

Does RAG reduce hallucinations in LLMs?

## Methodology

This report systematically synthesizes empirical metrics, theoretical claims, and technical analyses provided across five distinct research sources. The methodology compares operational performance improvements against structural and mathematical limitations of the RAG framework.

## Key Findings

- RAG significantly mitigates the frequency of hallucinations by anchoring outputs in retrieved external context, showing strong quantitative improvements in structured generation tasks.
- RAG does not completely eliminate hallucinations; LLMs can still generate misinformation if they misinterpret retrieved context or hallucinate around factually correct source material.
- From a theoretical perspective, RAG only improves semantic relevance and domain specificity without addressing the core reasoning failures or sequence likelihood optimizations that cause hallucinations internally.
- Conventional RAG effectiveness is highly constrained by input data quality, prompting advanced approaches like GenAI Data Fusion to combine structured and unstructured data to better combat hallucinations.

## Evidence & Analysis

Empirical evidence from Vectara shows that without a retriever, the percentage of hallucinated steps and tables can reach 21% on the 'Human Eval' split, whereas utilizing a retriever decreases this rate to less than 7.5% for steps and less than 4.5% for tables when using StarCoderBase LLMs.

Mathematical and structural analysis from Medium argues that RAG's retrieval phase relies on semantic similarity (e.g., cosine similarity), which cannot distinguish between factual truth and inaccuracy. Additionally, the generative phase optimizes for sequence likelihood without direct factual validation constraints.

Evidence from Wikipedia and arXiv notes that although RAG helps reduce hallucinations and minimizes retraining costs, it remains an indirect solution. LLMs can still generate misinformation by misinterpreting correct source context or fabricating details around the retrieved text.

According to K2view, conventional RAG systems remain prone to hallucinations because they rely heavily on unstructured, general data. They suggest that integrating structured and unstructured data from private sources (GenAI Data Fusion) is necessary to enhance RAG's effectiveness.

## Limitations

- The source pool lacks highly verified, peer-reviewed scientific studies, with all source quality indicators rated at 0.00.
- There is a lack of standardized benchmark results (e.g., HaluEval, TruthfulQA) comparing LLM outputs with and without RAG across diverse, unstructured domains.
- No clear distinction is maintained in the provided sources between source-faithfulness (faithfulness to the retrieved context) and external factual correctness.
- Quantitative data on hallucination reduction is highly domain-specific, focusing primarily on workflow step and table generation.

## Conclusion

RAG does reduce the operational occurrence of hallucinations in LLMs by providing relevant context, showing clear quantitative reductions in error rates for specific tasks. However, RAG does not mathematically or structurally eliminate hallucinations. Because RAG only improves contextual relevance and does not resolve the underlying probabilistic selection and reasoning flaws inherent in the generative phase of LLMs, models can still hallucinate around or misinterpret correct retrieved information. Therefore, RAG is a powerful hallucination mitigation technique, but it is not a direct or absolute solution.
